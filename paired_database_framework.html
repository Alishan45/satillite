Technical Brief & Implementation Framework
Satellite Data Assimilation Research

FY-4A + NWP Paired Sample Database

Target Region: East China (20°–45°N, 100°–135°E) Observation: FY-4A AGRI L1 Radiance Background: ERA5 Reanalysis
The Task: Build a paired sample database in which each sample links a numerical model state to a FY-4A satellite image, to be used for studying how information in satellite imagery can be assimilated into a numerical weather prediction model.
1. What would you pair with what? Why those two things?

I would pair top-of-atmosphere (TOA) brightness temperatures from FY-4A AGRI infrared (IR) and water vapor channels with the 3D thermodynamic, kinematic, and hydrometeor state of the NWP model across standard pressure levels (1000 to 250 hPa), supplemented by key 2D surface parameters.

Satellite Observations:

  • Channel 9 (6.25 µm): High-sensitivity absorption channel sensing upper-to-mid tropospheric water vapor.
  • Channel 10 (7.1 µm): Mid-to-lower tropospheric moisture; paired with C09 as a differential index (C09 - C10), it retrieves vertical moisture lapse rates.
  • Window Channels C12 (10.7 µm) & C13 (12.0 µm): Clean and split-window channels for cloud-top and skin temperature; the split difference (C12 - C13) diagnoses boundary-layer moisture, aerosols, and cloud optical depth.
  • CO₂ Slicing (C14, 13.5 µm): Resolves vertical atmospheric transmission for accurate Cloud Top Height (CTH) and semi-transparent cirrus detection.
  • Temporal Radiance Tendency (ΔT_b / 15 min): Differencing consecutive 15-minute scans captures rapid cloud-top cooling (< -10 K / 15 min), the signature of convective storm initiation.
  • Viewing & Solar Geometry: Satellite zenith/azimuth angles (airmass optical path length) and solar zenith angle.

NWP Model State: In addition to thermodynamic variables (T, q, u, v, z), modern all-sky assimilation requires cloud microphysics:

  • Cloud Liquid Water (q_l) & Cloud Ice Water (q_i) per pressure level (kg/kg).
  • Rain and Snow Water Content (q_r, q_s) and Fractional Cloud Cover (cc).
  • Column integrals and surface conditions: Surface Pressure (P_s), 2m Temperature (T_2m), Skin Temperature (T_skin), Total Column Water Vapor (TCWV), Total Column Cloud Liquid (TCLW), Land-Sea Mask (lsm), and Surface Orography (DEM).

Why Pair These Variables: In physical data assimilation, observed radiances are non-linear vertical integrals of temperature, moisture, and hydrometeor profiles governed by Radiative Transfer Models (RTM). Pairing multi-spectral IR/WV radiances directly targets the prognostic state variables updated by variational (3D/4D-Var) or ensemble Kalman filter schemes. Furthermore, IR/WV channels operate continuously 24/7 independent of daylight. Including cloud hydrometeors transitions the database from restrictive clear-sky assumptions to full all-sky assimilation, allowing AI models to learn direct mappings from satellite radiances to cloud water and precipitation.

2. How would you decide that a pair is correctly matched? What criterion would you use, and how large an error would you consider acceptable?

A pair is declared valid if and only if it satisfies three rigorous criteria across time, space, and physical integrity:

  • Temporal Matching: The FY-4A scan timestamp must align with the NWP model validity time within ≤ 15 minutes (hard ceiling < 30 minutes). Because convective clouds, frontal moisture boundaries, and vertical updrafts evolve on sub-hourly timescales, larger discrepancies produce unphysical representativeness error. Matching 5–15 minute satellite scans to hourly NWP fields yields an expected average offset of ~11–15 minutes (11.85 min achieved in our prototype), refinable via First Guess at Appropriate Time (FGAT) or 4D temporal interpolation.
  • Spatial Registration & Resolution Alignment: The native FY-4A geostationary projection (~4 km nadir) is regridded to the NWP regular lat-lon grid (0.25° × 0.25° ≈ 28 km) using area-weighted spatial aggregation. The acceptable spatial registration error is < 0.125° (< 14 km)—half a model grid cell.
  • Physical Bounds & Quality Control: Samples are rejected if missing/corrupted pixels exceed 5% within the analysis domain, or if calibrated brightness temperatures fall outside the physical atmospheric window of [170 K, 340 K]. NWP missing data must not exceed 5%.

4. In your view, which step of this task is the hardest, and why?

In my view, the hardest step is reconciling spatial representativeness and cloud parallax displacement when linking geostationary satellite radiances to grid-averaged NWP model columns.

1. The Parallax Geometry Problem: Because FY-4A is stationed at 104.7°E above the equator, line-of-sight paths intersect mid-latitude domains (East China, 20°–45°N) at oblique zenith angles of 40°–60°. High convective cloud towers reaching 12–16 km altitude are displaced laterally on the projection plane by 15 to 40 km away from their true ground nadir position. If uncorrected, cold cloud-top radiances map to the wrong vertical atmospheric column in the NWP state, injecting artificial dipole innovation errors into the assimilation system.

2. Non-Linear Multi-Scale Representativeness: Satellite infrared radiometers sample continuous sub-grid cloud heterogeneity at ~4 km, whereas NWP models represent discrete volumetric averages at ~28 km (0.25°). Radiative transfer through a partly cloudy grid cell is fundamentally non-linear: the average brightness temperature does not equal the radiative transfer of the average atmospheric state (E[RTM(x)] ≠ RTM(E[x])). Aggregating satellite radiances to the model grid without smoothing out sharp localized physical gradients (such as convective cores and drylines) is the primary physical and computational bottleneck.

Prototype Pipeline Validation: To test this framework in practice, I built a modular Python pipeline and validated it on real FY-4A AGRI Level-1 HDF observations and matching ERA5 atmospheric states over East China (20°–45°N, 100°–135°E). The pipeline successfully calibrated raw counts using instrument Look-Up Tables, performed geostationary inverse coordinate remapping, and generated a clean dataset of 17 paired samples (100% valid, 0.0% missing data in domain, mean Δt = 11.85 min) partitioned chronologically into training, validation, and test sets.
